# The research behind Mellow

## Why do we suggest these actions?

Every suggestion in Mellow comes from research on depression, not from guesswork. After each PHQ-9, we look at which areas are struggling and suggest small, everyday versions of approaches that studies have shown to help. Most studies tested full programmes, so we say our suggestions are *based on* this research, not proven by it.

| If this is hard... | We suggest | Based on |
|---|---|---|
| Interest and pleasure | Small planned activities you used to enjoy | Behavioural activation, [Ekers et al. 2014](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1428026) |
| Low mood | Writing down 3 good things from the day | [Seligman et al. 2005](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/LDBS2011.pdf) |
| Sleep | A fixed wake-up time, phone away at night | CBT for insomnia, [Freeman et al. 2017](https://www.clinician.com/articles/143218-chicken-or-egg-does-improving-sleep-improve-mental-health) |
| Energy | A 10-minute walk, light stretching | Exercise for depression, [Noetel et al. 2024](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/77895) |
| Appetite | Regular, simple meals | [Jacka et al. 2017](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/edsdoj__edsdoj.0908d2326619471a9fc8ab496e38b2cb) |
| Self-worth | Self-compassion exercises | [Ferrari et al. 2019](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/self-compassion-interventions-and-psychosocial-outcomes-a-meta-a/16630636) |
| Concentration | Better sleep and movement (no strong study for a single trick) | Same as sleep and energy |
| Feeling slowed down or restless | Movement, or 5 minutes of slow breathing | [Balban et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947) |
| Thoughts of self-harm | No self-help: crisis resources and a call from the clinic | Safety planning, [Stanley et al. 2018](https://sprc.org/?p=11015) |

## What do we track, and why?

We only track things that research links to depression, and always compare you with your own usual, never with other people.

- **Sleep** — irregular sleep is one of the earliest signs of relapse. In people recovering from depression, increasingly irregular sleep nearly doubled the risk of it coming back. ([HT World](https://www.htworld.co.uk/?p=48089), [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/new-study-offers-even-more-reasons-to-keep-to-a-regular-sleep-pattern))
- **Heart (resting heart rate and heart rate variability)** — depression is linked to lower heart rate variability, and the more severe the depression, the lower it gets. ([Kemp et al. 2010](https://www.pubmed.gov/20138254/))
- **Daily steps** — people who walk more have fewer depressive symptoms; even 1,000 extra steps a day makes a difference. ([Bizzozero-Peroni et al. 2024](https://epocrates.com/online/article/higher-daily-step-counts-may-reduce-depression-risk-in-adults))
- **Screen time** — more time on the phone was linked to more depressive symptoms. ([Saeb et al. 2015](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2015/07/your-phone-knows-if-youre-depressed))
- **How you feel (daily check-in) and the PHQ-9 every 2 weeks** — short daily mood questions closely follow the PHQ-9, the questionnaire clinicians already use. ([Burchert et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7787464/))

We left blood pressure out on purpose: studies on it and depression disagree, and most watches don't measure it well.
